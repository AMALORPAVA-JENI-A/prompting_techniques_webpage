Stop Conditions

What are Stop Conditions?

Stop conditions tell the AI when to stop generating output. They prevent overlong, repetitive, or irrelevant responses. Stop conditions help control the scope and length of AI responses.

Key Questions Stop Conditions Answer

  • When should the AI stop writing?
  • How much output is needed?
  • What is the ending point?

What to Include as Stop Conditions

  • Maximum length: Number of sentences, words, or items
  • Specific ending point: "Stop at conclusion" or "Stop after 5 points"
  • Completion triggers: "Stop after list is complete" or "Stop when summary is done"
  • Depth limits: "Stay at surface level" or "Provide 3 levels of detail"

How to Write Stop Conditions Clearly

  • Be precise and specific: "Stop after 5 points" not "stop soon"
  • Combine with output format for clarity
  • Place stop conditions at the end of instructions
  • Use numbers for quantifiable limits
  • Make conditions measurable and unambiguous

Best Practices

  • Prevent AI from generating unnecessary content
  • Combine with rules for better control
  • Keep instructions simple and clear
  • Use for multi-step tasks to avoid rambling
  • Test boundaries to find optimal lengths

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not specifying any stopping point
  • Being too vague ("Stop when ready")
  • Forgetting stop conditions in multi-step tasks
  • Setting unrealistic limits
  • Not explaining why a limit exists

Example

Without stop condition:

"List KPIs." → AI may list 20+ points

With stop condition:

"List 5 KPIs for a sales dashboard used by managers to track team performance."

Simple Stop Condition Template

Role: [role]
Task: [what to do]
Stop Condition: [when to stop]
Output: [format]
